\begin{abstract}
Software monoculture means one exploit generalises across every deployment of
a program. N-version diversity counters this by shipping structurally distinct
binaries that behave identically, but producing those variants by hand does not
scale. We present the clone-generation layer of AXIOM, a system that produces
program variants which are structurally different, behaviourally identical, and
close to a caller-specified size. The layer composes a catalogue of 17
semantics-preserving transformations under a length-targeted beam search adapted
from Auto-SPT, and gates every candidate through a four-layer equivalence oracle
built on recorded golden vectors rather than tests alone. Across 24
(module,~target-size) cells on a four-module industrial control corpus, the
search explored 2{,}398 candidates and returned an oracle-verified variant in
24/24 cells, with control-flow structure altered in 16/24. On a second,
independently authored corpus the layer diversified 12 of 18 runs with 11
accepted by the oracle, restructuring control flow entirely on three of six
programs, though a seeding defect in that build means those runs do not yet
demonstrate migration and diversification composed end to end. Growing the catalogue
from 12 to 17 rules raised mean rename-insensitive diversity by 73\% and
improved length accuracy. An ablation on the ranking metric changed the selected
variant in only 3/24 cells and never changed length accuracy, which we trace to
the lexicographic objective. We report these as preliminary results and are
explicit about what the oracle does and does not establish.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A vulnerability in a widely deployed program is a vulnerability in every
installation of it. This is the software monoculture problem, and the classical
answer is N-version programming: run several implementations that agree on
behaviour but differ in structure, so that a memory-corruption exploit tuned to
one layout fails on the others. The obstacle has always been cost. Writing
independent implementations is expensive, and automated diversification has
historically operated at the binary or compiler level, where the transformations
are opaque to the developer and hard to audit.

This paper describes the component of AXIOM that generates such variants at the
\emph{source} level. Three properties make the problem non-trivial:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Equivalence must be checked, not assumed.} A transformation that
      is semantics-preserving in principle can still be misapplied. Program
      equivalence is undecidable in general (Rice's theorem), so we do not claim
      proof; we claim equivalence \emph{with respect to a stated set of checks},
      and we state them.
\item \textbf{Diversity must survive the obvious objection.} A variant produced
      by renaming identifiers is not diverse in any useful sense. Our ranking
      metric is computed after discarding every identifier, so renaming cannot
      inflate it.
\item \textbf{Size is a deployment constraint.} A variant that is three times
      the size of the original may not fit the target. We treat a caller-supplied
      line target as the primary search objective, with diversity as a
      tie-break --- not the reverse.
\end{enumerate}

This is a preliminary report on one layer of a larger system. We report what we
measured, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} states plainly what these numbers do not
establish.

\section{Approach}

\subsection{Transformation catalogue}

The catalogue holds 17 semantics-preserving transformations (SPTs), of which the
four LLM-generated rules are opt-in: they are loaded only when the caller asks
for them, so the standalone sweeps of Section~\ref{sec:catalogue} use all 17
while the pipeline deployment of Section~\ref{sec:deploy} used the 13
hand-written rules alone. Thirteen are
hand-written using LibCST, a format-preserving concrete syntax tree library:
transforming through a CST rather than an AST means every part of the file the
transformation did not touch comes back byte-identical, so a reviewer reads a
minimal diff instead of a whole-file reformat. Four further rules were
LLM-generated from a specification and then subjected to the same oracle as
every other rule.

Rules fall into three groups. \emph{Statement-level} rules change expression
structure without touching control flow (\code{split\_return\_expression},
\code{extract\_subexpression}, \code{expand\_aug\_assign}).
\emph{Control-flow} rules change the branch graph (\code{ternary\_to\_if\_else},
\code{invert\_if\_else}, \code{demorgan\_branch\_swap},
\code{for\_range\_to\_while}, \code{merge\_nested\_if}). \emph{Binding} rules
change names and temporaries (\code{rename\_locals},
\code{inline\_single\_use\_return}).

The distinction matters because the two groups move different metrics, and a
catalogue weighted only toward the first produces variants that are longer but
structurally unchanged --- a failure mode we observed directly and address in
Section~\ref{sec:catalogue}.

not in conflict --- they answer different questions, which is why we report
several rather than one, and why \code{composite} weights both equally.

\subsection{Four-layer equivalence oracle}

Every candidate is checked before it may enter the beam. All four layers must
pass, evaluated cheapest-first in a scratch copy of the program, in a
subprocess, so a candidate that hangs or corrupts state cannot affect the next:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Import integrity} --- every first-party module still imports.
\item \textbf{Golden regression} --- every probe reproduces its recorded value
      \emph{exactly}. Tolerance is zero; float reassociation is therefore
      forbidden, which is deliberate for control code.
\item \textbf{Invariants} --- properties that must hold for \emph{any} input,
      swept over ranges rather than checked at points.
\item \textbf{Coverage} --- the program's own test suite still passes.
\end{enumerate}

Golden vectors are recorded once by executing the \emph{pristine} program and
frozen. They are never re-recorded after a rewrite: an oracle that re-derives
its answer key from the code under test agrees with whatever it is given, which
is the one failure mode a golden-vector scheme must not have. Probes are chosen
for branch coverage rather than representativeness, since a golden set that
never enters a fault path cannot detect a broken fault path.

Invariants are validated against the pristine program \emph{before} being
trusted. This caught a real defect in our own harness: an early invariant
asserted that a counts-to-level conversion clamped to $[0, 10]$, which the
original does not do --- it returns $-1.22$ for $-500$ counts. An invariant the
original violates rejects a perfect copy, and the oracle would have looked
rigorous while accepting nothing.

\subsection{Diversity metrics}

We report five metrics, of which two are rename-insensitive and are the only
ones we make claims from.

\code{ast\_structural} is the ranking signal: the pre-order sequence of AST node
\emph{types}, with all identifiers discarded, compared by sequence alignment,
reported as $1 - \mathrm{ratio}$. A histogram of node types proved far too
insensitive at this scale --- extracting a subexpression from an 89-line module
moved a cosine distance by ${\sim}0.0003$, which cannot rank anything --- so the
comparison is order-aware.

\code{cfg\_shape} counts control-flow constructs tagged by nesting depth
(\code{If@0}, \code{For@1}) and takes a weighted Jaccard distance. It is
identifier-free, so branch restructuring registers and renaming does not.

\code{composite} is $0.5\cdot\code{ast\_structural} + 0.5\cdot\code{cfg\_shape}$.
The equal weighting is deliberate: \code{ast\_structural} alone rewards
statement-level padding without ever altering control flow.

\code{ncd} is normalised compression distance~\cite{ncd}, used unmodified with
zlib as the compressor. \code{du\_chain} is a Jaccard distance~\cite{jaccard}
over define--use pairs. Both are explicitly rename-sensitive and we do not rank
by them.

\paragraph{Provenance of these metrics.} The ingredients are established;
the specific measures are ours, and we state which is which because the
distinction affects how much weight the numbers can carry. Normalised
compression distance and the Jaccard index are standard and used as published.
\code{ast\_structural} composes two standard pieces --- AST node-type
sequences and Ratcliff/Obershelp sequence matching~\cite{ratcliff} --- but the
composition is our own. \code{du\_chain} approximates define--use chains by
collecting \emph{(function, used-name)} pairs rather than performing
reaching-definitions analysis. \code{cfg\_shape} is not a control-flow graph in
the formal sense: it has no basic blocks and no edges, only a depth-tagged
histogram of branch and loop constructs. \code{composite} weights its two terms
equally by judgement, not by fitting.

Consequently none of these is validated against an external referent. We can
show that a variant differs structurally by these measures; we cannot yet show
that the difference predicts anything an attacker or a clone detector would
care about. Correlating them against an independent clone detector --- the
objective Auto-SPT optimises directly --- is the cheapest way to ground them
and is planned work.

\subsection{Length-targeted beam search}

The search skeleton is Auto-SPT's Algorithm 1 --- maintain a beam of the $B$
best candidates and iterate expand, filter, select --- with its clone-detector
objective replaced by a size objective. Each iteration applies every rule to
every beam member, discards candidates that are not valid Python, submits the
remainder to the oracle, and sorts the survivors by

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{key}(c) = \bigl(\,\lvert \mathrm{lines}(c) - T \rvert,\; -D(x, c)\,\bigr)
\label{eq:key}
\end{equation}

where $T$ is the requested line target and $D$ the ranking metric. The key is a
tuple compared lexicographically. Diversity can therefore never buy back a
length miss; it only orders candidates already tied on length. This is what
keeps the length control honest, and it is the substantive departure from
Auto-SPT, whose objective is a clone-detector score.

The search stops when
\begin{equation}
\lvert \mathrm{lines}(c^{*}) - T \rvert \;\le\; \max\bigl(1,\ \lceil \lvert T - \mathrm{lines}(x) \rvert \cdot \tau \rceil \bigr)
\label{eq:tol}
\end{equation}
with $\tau = 0.10$. Tolerance scales with the requested \emph{change}, not the
file size. Sizing it off the absolute count makes it meaningless: a 10\% band on
a 97-line target is $\pm 9$ lines, so a search asked to add 8 lines could add
none and stop satisfied.

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Corpora}

The primary corpus is four industrial-control modules (89--134 LOC): a PID
governor, a pump controller, a protective relay, and a sensor pipeline. Its
oracle holds 1{,}904 recorded observations across the four modules.

The second corpus is an independently authored set of twelve industrial-control
programs in four categories, of which categories C and D specify that
first-party logic must be diversified in addition to any library substitution.
Categories A and B specify substitution only, with no logic mutation, and are
therefore outside this layer's scope; the six C/D programs are the ones this
paper uses. We authored a golden-vector oracle for those six, holding 170
recorded observations, following the same four-layer construction described in
Section~2.2. This corpus is the deployment check of
Section~\ref{sec:deploy}: it was not written by us, and it exercises the layer
inside the full pipeline rather than standalone.

Each module is swept at six size ratios ($0.85$, $0.90$, $1.00$, $1.10$,
$1.20$, $1.30$ of original length), giving 24 (module,~target) cells. Beam size
3, at most 6 iterations.

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the sweep. The search explored 2{,}398 candidates
and returned an oracle-verified variant in \textbf{24/24} cells, in 395\,s of
total search time (4.83 iterations per cell on average).

Two figures deserve comment rather than celebration. First, all 2{,}398
candidates that reached the oracle passed it. This is what one expects when the
transformations are semantics-preserving by construction, but it does mean the
sweep provides no evidence that the oracle \emph{discriminates}. We establish
that separately: injecting a deliberate fault into an otherwise-correct module
is rejected at the import layer, and in pipeline deployment the same oracle
rejected 3 of 18 runs (Section~\ref{sec:deploy}). Second, only 5/24 cells hit
their line target exactly, with a mean absolute error of 8.88 lines. Length
control is best-effort, and we report the miss rather than reporting only the
cells that landed.

\code{protective\_relay} is the clearest negative result: highest
\code{ast\_structural} (0.1000) but lowest \code{cfg\_shape} (0.0556) and the
worst length error. It has no shrink path in the current catalogue --- only two
rules can remove lines --- so every sub-unity ratio for that module is
unreachable.

Table~\ref{tab:catalogue} isolates the effect of the catalogue. Adding the four
LLM-generated rules and then one hand-written rule raised mean
rename-insensitive diversity by \textbf{73\%} and cut mean length error from
12.50 to 8.88 lines. The gain is superlinear in rule count because rules compose:
each new rule multiplies the reachable space rather than adding to it.

The single hand-written addition, \code{for\_range\_to\_while}, was motivated by
a specific failure. Under the 12-rule catalogue most winning variants scored
\code{cfg\_shape}${}=0$ --- structurally identical control flow --- which
undermines the entire premise. An earlier attempt, \code{elif\_to\_nested\_if},
scored exactly $0$ because \code{elif} and \code{else: if} are indistinguishable
in the Python AST. Rewriting counted \code{for} loops as explicit \code{while}
loops does change the graph, and control-flow structure is now altered in 16/24
cells.

\subsection{Ranking-metric ablation}

We re-ran the full sweep with the ranking metric changed from
\code{ast\_structural} to \code{composite}, holding everything else fixed. The
selected variant differed in \textbf{3 of 24} cells, and achieved line counts
were \emph{identical in 24/24}.

This is the behaviour Equation~\ref{eq:key} predicts: because the key is
lexicographic and length dominates, changing the ranking metric can only reorder
candidates that are already tied on length. The bottleneck on this corpus is the
reachable set --- what the catalogue can produce --- not the choice among
what it produces. We report this as a negative result. It also means the metric
choice is safe to revisit later without invalidating length-accuracy claims.

The layer runs as an agent inside AXIOM's migration pipeline, invoked on
first-party logic after a library substitution. These runs used the 13
hand-written rules only; the four generated rules are opt-in and the pipeline
did not request them, so this deployment exercises a smaller catalogue than

Three results matter here. First, \textbf{three of six programs reached}
\code{cfg\_shape}${}={}$\textbf{1.0} --- control-flow structure entirely
restructured, with every recorded probe value still reproducing exactly. Mean
composite diversity on this corpus (0.4631) is substantially higher than on the
primary corpus (0.0968), because these programs are smaller (25--38 LOC), so
each applied rule moves a larger fraction of the structure. That is a property
of program size, not evidence that the layer works better here.

Second, the \textbf{single oracle rejection} --- one D1 seed of three --- is the
discriminating evidence the main sweep lacks. The same rule chain that the
oracle accepted on two seeds it rejected on the third.

Third, C1 and C2 produced \textbf{no transformations at all}. Both are cases
where the specified target is not an installable package but a
reimplementation, and the upstream resolver stage returns no substitution to
diversify; the layer is never reached with anything to work on. This is a
pipeline-integration limit rather than a limit of the catalogue.

\paragraph{Refusal as a measurable outcome.} Two further programs in the same
corpus specify a migration that cannot be written: both ask for
\code{pymodbus}${\rightarrow}$\code{minimalmodbus}, but the originals speak
Modbus over TCP and \code{minimalmodbus} is serial-only, exposing no TCP entry
point at all. The correct behaviour is to decline.

them as negative controls: correct means \emph{not} completing the
substitution. Table~\ref{tab:controls} separates the two populations.

The full pipeline declined all six control runs with a recorded stage failure
--- three at the substitution agent, three at the verifier. The
no-verification ablation also declined all six, but recorded no reason for any
of them, which is a materially weaker result: an arm that does nothing on every
program would score full marks on negative controls, so outcome alone is cheap
credit and the ``w/ reason'' column is what separates deliberate refusal from
inactivity. The single-prompt baseline completed the impossible substitution in
all six runs, and our un-injected factory check flags five of those six as
raising on first real call.

We report this because a system that refuses cannot demonstrate that refusal in
a success-rate table --- a correct decline and an outright failure are
indistinguishable there. It is also the clearest evidence in this paper that
the checking apparatus rejects rather than rubber-stamps. Two caveats: this is
a re-scoring of existing runs rather than new data, and the criterion was
chosen after observing the results. It rests on a pre-existing column
(``did the substitution complete''), and the impossibility is verifiable from
the target library's own signature, neither of which depends on how any arm
scored.

\paragraph{A seeding defect these runs exposed.} The numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:deploy} were produced by a build in which the search seeded from
the corpus's \emph{pristine} module rather than from the patched module handed
to it by the preceding stage. Two consequences follow. The library substitution
was discarded, so the variants measured here are diversified originals rather
than diversified migrations. And the line target, computed from the patched
text, described a different file than the one searched --- in the worst case a
25-line module was given a 167-line target and reported a 138-line miss, which
is why we report no length-accuracy figures for this corpus. We have since
fixed the seeding; on a re-run of one program the search now starts from the
patched source, retains the substitution, and lands within one line of target.
The diversity and equivalence results in Table~\ref{tab:deploy} stand as
measurements of the catalogue and oracle, but they do not yet demonstrate
length control or migration-plus-diversification composed end to end. That
composition is the first experiment we intend to repeat.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{We do not prove equivalence.} Program equivalence is undecidable. Every
claim here is equivalence with respect to four stated checks. A rewrite that
breaks a path neither the probes nor the tests visit would pass.

\textbf{The oracle inherits its corpus's blind spots.} Both corpora inject test
doubles at construction sites, so probes that follow the same pattern do not
execute the un-injected path. We are aware of this and treat it as future work
rather than a solved problem.

\textbf{The metrics are not externally validated.} Two of the five are standard
and used as published; \code{ast\_structural}, \code{cfg\_shape} and
\code{composite} are our own constructions, and nothing yet ties them to an
outcome an attacker or a clone detector would recognise. A high
\code{cfg\_shape} means the branch histogram moved, no more than that.

\textbf{Scale.} Four modules and 24 cells is a pilot; the second corpus adds six
programs. The 73\% and 16/24 figures are measurements on the primary corpus, not
general claims, and the two corpora differ enough in program size that their
diversity values are not directly comparable.

\textbf{Shrinking is weak.} Only two rules remove lines, so sub-unity targets
are frequently unreachable, which dominates the 8.88-line mean error.

\textbf{No attack evaluation.} We measure structural difference, not exploit
resistance. Whether these variants defeat a real memory-corruption exploit is
the obvious next experiment and is not attempted here.

\section{Related work}

Auto-SPT~\cite{autospt} provides the beam-search skeleton and the notion of
composing semantics-preserving transformations under a search objective; we
replace its clone-detector objective with a size target, which changes what the
search optimises for. Classical N-version programming motivates the goal;
compiler-level and binary-level diversification pursue it at a layer where the
transformation is not auditable by the developer. MigrateLib~\cite{migratelib}
informs our use of format-preserving rewriting for minimal diffs.

\section{Conclusion}

We described AXIOM's clone-generation layer and reported preliminary results: an
oracle-verified variant in 24/24 cells over 2{,}398 explored candidates, control
flow altered in 16/24, a 73\% diversity gain from growing the catalogue from 12
to 17 rules, and a ranking-metric ablation that returned a clean negative
result. The immediate next steps are shrink-capable transformations, probes that
exercise un-injected construction paths, and an attack-based evaluation to test
whether measured structural difference translates into exploit resistance.
